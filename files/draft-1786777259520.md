If you're here, you've probably already read my **About Me** page.

I'm a developer and a hobbyist robotics engineer. I've never particularly liked writing blogs—I barely managed to get an A in IGCSE English. We don't talk about high school. :)

But I've come to believe that if I want to build things worth talking about, I should also have a place to talk about them.

After a humbling experience trying to crack the JEE—the final boss of Indian exams—in just six months, and after applying to Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, I'm back with renewed vigor.

My long-term goal is to get into **ETH Zurich** for a Master's degree in **Robotics, Systems and Control**.

To get there, I have four years of a B.Tech ahead of me. Four years to learn robotics, build ridiculous projects, make plenty of mistakes, and hopefully become a much better engineer than I am today.

And that's where this blog comes in.

## Why write?

I don't want this blog to be a glorified CV.

I don't want to publish posts just so I can point at them during an application and say, _"Look, I have a blog."_

Instead, I want it to be a record of the journey.

Whenever I learn something interesting—whether it's PID control, computer vision, embedded systems, reinforcement learning, a robotics paper, or some weird piece of hardware—I want to try explaining it.

Because explaining something is a surprisingly good way of finding out whether you actually understand it.

The process is simple:

**Learn → Research → Build → Explain → Share**

Sometimes the thing I learn will turn into a project. Sometimes a project will turn into a post. Sometimes I'll discover that I understood something far less than I thought I did.

That's fine.

The point isn't to pretend that I already know everything.

The point is to document myself learning.

## What can you expect?

Most of what I write here will revolve around robotics, AI, and engineering.

I'll probably write about things like:

- Robotics and autonomous systems
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Computer vision
- Control systems
- Embedded systems
- Electronics
- Sensors and actuators
- Interesting research papers
- New technologies and engineering developments
- Whatever else manages to grab my attention

As I progress through university, I expect the content to become more technical too.

Right now, I'll probably be writing about fundamentals and things I'm discovering for the first time.

A couple of years from now, I'd like to be writing about things like estimation, optimization, manipulation, perception, and advanced control—and, ideally, have the projects to back them up.

## Why make it public?

There are plenty of places I could keep notes privately.

But there's something different about putting your work where other people can see it.

It forces me to be more precise. It gives me a reason to finish things. It creates a record of what I was interested in, what I built, what I got wrong, and how my thinking changed.

And maybe, somewhere along the way, someone else finds something useful here.

That's a pretty good reason to write.

## The bigger picture

My academics come first.

The blog isn't supposed to consume my weekends or become another source of pressure. If I have an exam on Monday and a blog post due on Sunday, the blog loses.

The real goal is to spend these four years becoming an excellent engineer.

The blog is just the trail of breadcrumbs.

If I do this properly, four years from now I won't just have a collection of articles. I'll have a public record of four years of learning, experimenting, building, failing, and improving.

And hopefully, a lot of robots.

So that's what this is.

I'm not a writer.

I'm an engineer who is going to try writing.

Let's see how that goes.

And welcome to the odyssey.
