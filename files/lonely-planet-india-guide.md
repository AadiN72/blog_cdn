I still remember the first time I opened a Lonely Planet guide and felt like I had the whole world in my hands.

The 2003 India guide is a strange little artifact now: it is part travel manual, part time capsule, and part reminder that the internet was not always the default way to plan a trip. The pages smelled like paper and possibility. Every map had a story. Every recommendation felt earned.

It reminded me that travel writing is not just about logistics. It is about how a place gets framed in your head before you have even arrived. The best guidebooks do not simply tell you where to go; they teach you how to notice.

That is still what I look for in technology writing too: not a checklist, but a lens.
