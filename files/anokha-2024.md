Anokha was the kind of event that reminds you how much engineering energy exists in a campus setting when it is given room to breathe.

There were robots, demos, student projects, and the kind of technical enthusiasm that feels contagious. It was a good counterweight to the usual abstract talk about innovation. Here, the work was physical, visible, and deeply hands-on.

What struck me most was how much creativity there was around simple ideas. A machine that was just a little polished could instantly become a conversation piece. It made the entire ecosystem feel alive.

I left feeling more energized than I had before, which is usually the best sign that a technical event is actually doing its job.
